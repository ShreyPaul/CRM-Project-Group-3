## Our concern
If employees are aware of constant monitoring, even honest employees will change their behaviour to avoid being falsely detected by the AI. They may use fewer official channels to voice concerns, weakening the culture of direct reporting — which creates risk in itself, including cyber risk.

## Evidence

**Ravid et al. (2023)** — Meta-analysis of 94 samples and 23,461 participants: electronic performance monitoring (EPM) was associated with more employee stress; more transparent, less invasive monitoring went with better attitudes.
*Our comment:* This covers performance monitoring, not insider-threat tools, but it supports tracking stress and satisfaction in our model.

**Bernstein (2012)** — Factory study ("transparency paradox"): being observed pushed workers to hide what they were doing.
*Our comment:* Fits our concern that employees will move concerns away from official channels.

**Thiel et al. (2023)** — Monitoring lowered employees' sense of agency and increased workplace deviance; perceived fairness weakened the effect.
*Our comment:* Bears directly on our grievance-driven traitor scenario, though it is not evidence about bank insiders.

**Flatø & Lund-Tønnesen (2025)** — Norwegian survey linking electronic monitoring and turnover intention.
*Our comment:* Informs B's assumed 7.3-percentage-point turnover-intention gap (Document 4). Illustrative only; it does not measure causal BNY turnover.

**Axelsson (2000)** — Base-rate fallacy: when attacks are rare, most alerts can be false even with a good detector. Worked example: 10 malicious employees in 10,000, 90% detection, 1% false-positive rate → ~9 true alerts and ~100 false ones, so only ~8% of flags are real.
*Our comment:* False alarms carry a cost of their own; this motivates our precision concerns.

**MITRE Insider Threat Research & Solutions (n.d.)** — Language and sentiment analysis has not shown strong proactive detection and produces many irrelevant alerts.
*Our comment:* A direct caution against the vendor claims in Document 1.
