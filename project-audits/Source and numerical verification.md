# Source and numerical verification

This review compares the report's values with the current model and selected original sources. A calculation matching the model does not establish that its assumptions are empirically correct. The review identifies confirmed values, unsupported or conflicting claims, and items not fully checked; it does not establish whether any particular wording was AI-generated.

## Current model results

The baseline values in Documents 4 and 6 reproduce the current NumPy chart output: simulated means of $99.57M, $87.06M and $69.10M and P95s of $150.00M, $138.74M and $107.46M for A, B and C. The individual sensitivities ($61.11M, $58.96M and $79.86M for B) also match the output. The analytical expectations in the index and web explorer ($99.61M, $86.97M and $69.31M) are consistent with the same inputs. They are direct expectations, not the simulation means; the index now labels this distinction next to its simulated P95.

The component decomposition, $20,500 replacement cost and employee totals of $14,396,330 and $10,846,550 are arithmetically consistent with the stated assumptions. The break-even multiplier of approximately 68.5% also matches the model. These numbers are model outputs: the attempt rates, success probabilities, loss ranges, employee responses, costs and enforcement probabilities are scenario assumptions, not verified BNY observations. A first-year employee cost is included in each simulated annual total; it does not establish a recurring multi-year cost.

## External facts checked

- **BNY financial and employee values:** The 2025 annual report supports $59.3T assets under custody/administration, $2.178T assets under management, $20.080B revenue, $13.054B noninterest expense, $7.159B staff expense and 48,100 full-time employees. These describe 2025, not necessarily the company's current scale. [BNY annual report](https://prodcs.bny.com/assets/corporate/documents/pdf/investor-relations/annual-report-2025.pdf).
- **SEC penalty:** The SEC order supports the $40M joint-and-several penalty against BNY Mellon Securities Corporation and Pershing LLC for recordkeeping violations. The case does not establish the model's annual enforcement probabilities. [SEC order, section IV.D](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2024/34-100712.pdf).
- **Monitoring surveys:** The APA source supports the reported monitored/unmonitored percentages in Document 2, including 32%/24%, 45%/29%, 56%/approximately 40%, and job-search intention 42%/23%. These are associations in a survey, not estimates of monitoring's causal effect. [APA survey](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/work-in-america/2023-work-america-ai-monitoring). The Ravid meta-analysis supports 94 samples and 23,461 participants and the stated stress and attitude findings. [Original meta-analysis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/peps.12514).
- **7.3-point assumption:** The Norwegian study reports estimated turnover intention of 24.43% versus 17.13% for private-sector time-use monitoring, a difference of 7.30 percentage points. It is not a BNY-specific causal estimate or a comparison of regimes B and C. The correct author spelling is Flatøy, not Flatø. [Original paper](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/14719037.2025.2608281).
- **Alert example:** With 10 malicious employees among 10,000, 90% detection and a 1% false-positive rate, expected alerts are 9 true and 99.9 false; about 8.3% are true. This is correct as an illustrative calculation, not evidence of the model's actual detector performance.

## Claims requiring correction or stronger sourcing

1. **TWOS dataset (Document 1):** The original dataset paper supports 24 users across five days and approximately 18 hours of masquerader activity, but says it obtained *at least two* instances of traitor data. It does not support the report's specific claim of five traitor instances at 120 minutes each. The claimed counts and session durations should be removed or traced to a specified release and source. [Original TWOS paper](https://isyou.info/jowua/papers/jowua-v9n1-3.pdf).
2. **Living Security privacy claim (Document 1):** The linked article explicitly discusses balancing security with employee privacy and transparency, so the statement that it makes “no mention of privacy” is incorrect. A discussion of privacy is not proof that particular technical safeguards exist. [Living Security article](https://www.livingsecurity.com/blog/predict-insider-threats-ai).
3. **Proofpoint citation (Document 1):** The cited solution brief does not mention sentiment in its searchable text. Proofpoint has a separate product data sheet that does describe sentiment analysis; the report should cite that specific product and avoid treating a vendor claim as proof of accuracy. [Proofpoint Communications Insights data sheet](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/data-sheets/pfpt-us-ds-communications-insights-for-insider-threat-management.pdf).
4. **Dataset details (Document 1):** The CERT provider confirms the datasets are synthetic, but the report does not identify a source for all its release-specific counts, dates and activity figures. It states five insiders “of 3,995 users”; one research description gives 4,000 total users including five malicious users. The denominator needs clarification before quoting a prevalence. [CMU dataset page](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/insider-threat-test-dataset/); [research description](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/9/19/4018). The Enron corpus is commonly described as about 150 users and 500,000 messages, but its time span depends on the release; an academic description places it in 1998–2002, not exclusively 2000–2002. [CMU dataset](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~enron/); [dataset study](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/~yener/PAPERS/SECURITY/enron.pdf).
5. **Employee behaviour (Document 2):** The opening presents behavioural changes as certain. The cited studies provide reasons to investigate them, but do not establish that all employees will stop using official channels or that monitoring causes more insider attacks at BNY. Treat the channel-shifting and attack-attempt effects as hypotheses.
6. **ATT&CK coverage (Document 5):** The 16-entry counts match the project's figure table, not independent measured effectiveness. The Navigator files do not consistently match the table, and the script does not regenerate them. A blocked USB technique also coexists with nonzero modeled USB losses; the report needs to explain residual bypass or failure. See [project consistency audit](Project%20consistency%20audit.md).
7. **References and traceability:** Document 2 leaves Flatøy and Lund-Tønnesen's initials blank and misspells Flatøy. Document 1 explicitly lacks a complete dataset bibliography. Some listed vendor statements describe marketing claims; they are not independent effectiveness studies.
